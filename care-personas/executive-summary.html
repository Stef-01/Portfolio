Two care-model concepts, one page

Both redirect care to the clinician best placed to give it, pay that clinician properly for doing so, and are testable inside a year. All dollars indicative AUD, rounded from published MBS schedule fees; all rates are modelling assumptions the proposed trials exist to measure.

CONCEPT 01

The Specialist-GP Pipeline

For hospital networks & the Chief Medical Officer

The problem

A fifth of every specialist's appointment book is stable, protocol-governed follow-up — the most expensive clinician in the building doing the least complex work in it, while genuinely complex referrals queue 127 days behind them.

The mechanism

  • Score every referral and follow-up with a six-factor Complexity Screen (0–100).
  • The bottom quintile moves to accredited Specialist-GPs; the specialist keeps the rest.
  • An eConsult backstop returns specialist judgement in ≤72 h (24 h urgent), paid at $65.
  • Named specialist of record retained; annual re-screen moves patients both ways; escalation capped ≤15%/yr.

Generalises

Endocrinology, respiratory, gastroenterology and rheumatology all carry an equivalent bottom quintile (18–24%). Per 1,000 specialist FTE: 400,000 appointments redirected out of outpatients and 240,000 extra complex assessments, for ~$93M additional Medicare outlay against $116M network-side benefit — a throughput trade, argued as one.

The ask

$460k, 12 months, one 6-FTE department and ~24 accredited GPs. Payback ≈ 8 months on network benefit alone. Success = wait ≤75 days, eConsult resolution ≥85%, escalation ≤15%, no rise in 90-day ED representation.

CONCEPT 02

The Matched Family GP

For primary-care networks & payers

The problem

Roughly 40% of adults have no real usual-GP relationship — not from cost, but from fit. Only 52% of recommended chronic follow-ups happen, and ~560 appointments per GP FTE evaporate each year into gaps and no-shows.

The mechanism

  • Clinicians declare, at structured onboarding, which patients they want more of.
  • Patients answer a 20-second preference conversation; the algorithm finds the overlap.
  • Matched care lands: explained in the patient's first language, inside their food and beliefs — a plan understood is a plan followed.
  • Follow-up runs outbound: “your GP is free Thursday 2:40 — reply YES to book”.
  • Cadences come from EHR-predicted need (post-MI q3mo for year one, T2DM, postnatal, CKD).

Guardrails

Matching records who a clinician wants more of, never who they refuse — every doctor still sees every patient and urgent care is never routed through matching. Cadences are owned by clinical governance, not growth; data use is consented and revocable; bulk-billing is preserved for concession patients.

The ask

$380k, 6 months, three practices and ~30 GPs. Success = slot fill ≥98%, no-shows ≤3%, recall completion ≥70% at six months, message opt-out ≤10%, PREM “my doctor gets me” ≥85%.

Why the two belong together

Concept 01 gives general practice more complex, better-remunerated work; Concept 02 makes sure those patients are attached to a GP who fits them and actually come back. Run separately, each stands on its own numbers. Run together in one region, the pipeline supplies the complexity and the matching engine supplies the continuity — and the same $840k buys evidence for both.
