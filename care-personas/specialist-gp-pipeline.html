01 — The problem

Two workforces, both mispriced

Every outpatient clinic runs the same quiet inefficiency. The specialist — the most expensive, longest-trained clinician in the building — spends a fifth of the week on stable reviews a protocol could run. Meanwhile the GP, the clinician actually built for continuity, is priced into six-minute medicine by a fee schedule that rewards throughput over depth. Both are capable of more. Neither is paid for it.

02 — Segment the caseload, not the clinicians

One appointment book. Five personas.

Precision public health showed that one population is really five or six, each needing a different intervention. The same is true of a specialist's appointment book. Score every referral and every follow-up with a short Complexity Screen — diagnosis stability, device or procedure dependency, comorbidity count, admission history, titration status, red flags — and five personas appear. Two of them don't need a specialist most years. They need a well-supported GP.

The Steady State

12% of the book · 240 appts/yr
Who they are
Controlled AF on a DOAC, stable three years running. Blood pressure at target, echo unchanged. Comes in once or twice a year to be told they're fine.
What they need
A protocolised annual review, medicines continuity, risk-factor coaching, and a crisp list of triggers that mean “call the specialist”.
Who manages them
Today: specialist review every 6–12 months. Pipeline: accredited GP runs the annual cycle; eConsult if anything drifts.
→ Specialist-GP pipeline

The dollars: a $92 specialist review becomes a $535/yr structured GP care relationship — and frees a clinic slot for a new complex patient.

The Protocol Titration

8% of the book · 160 appts/yr
Who they are
Heart failure on guideline therapy that still needs stepping up. Post-ACS lipids at eighteen months. The plan is already written — it just needs executing, dose by dose.
What they need
Frequent, low-acuity touchpoints against a published algorithm, with pathology monitoring between visits.
Who manages them
Today: a specialist visit every three months for a dose change the protocol already specifies. Pipeline: GP titrates to protocol; eConsult at the decision points (~2 per year).
→ Specialist-GP pipeline

The dollars: more frequent GP care (Level C/D + care plan) bills ~$535/yr; the specialist is paid $65 per eConsult for the judgement calls only.

The Multimorbid Juggler

35% of the book · 700 appts/yr
Who they are
Heart failure plus stage-4 kidney disease plus diabetes plus frailty. Every adjustment to one system trades off another.
What they need
A specialist weighing genuine trade-offs, in structured shared care with a GP who holds the whole picture.
Who manages them
Specialist-led, before and after. Pipeline effect: with the book decongested, the specialist finally has the minutes these patients actually need.
Specialist core

The dollars: unchanged items, better used — longer, higher-value consultations instead of a corridor-speed clinic.

The Diagnostic Puzzle

20% of the book · 400 appts/yr
Who they are
Syncope, cause unknown. Exertional breathlessness with a clean first-line workup. Something is wrong and nobody knows what yet.
What they need
The full diagnostic skillset — and to be seen in weeks, not months, while the window for a good outcome is open.
Who manages them
Specialist, unambiguously. Pipeline effect: these are the patients the freed slots are for — 240 more of them seen per FTE, per year.
Specialist core

The dollars: each new complex assessment bills $170 and cascades the diagnostics — echo, Holter, stress testing — the department runs on.

The High-Stakes

25% of the book · 500 appts/yr
Who they are
TAVI workup. A device patient. Decompensating heart failure a fortnight out of hospital. The reason cardiologists trained for a decade.
What they need
Whole-of-skillset procedural and acute care, with no queue in front of it.
Who manages them
Specialist, always. Pipeline effect: faster access into the procedural stream that funds the service.
Specialist core

The dollars: the highest-margin work in the department — reached sooner because the bottom quintile no longer queues in front of it.

03 — The mechanism

One screen, two tracks, a backstop

Every new referral and every existing follow-up passes through the same Complexity Screen. The bottom quintile flows to accredited Specialist-GPs; everything else concentrates in the specialist core. An asynchronous eConsult channel means the GP is never more than 72 hours from specialist judgement — and every patient keeps a named specialist of record. Patients are re-screened annually and move in both directions.

06 — The ask

A 12-month trial: one department, one screen, hard numbers

One cardiology department (6 FTE), its referral catchment, and ~24 accredited GPs. Budget $460k: program management & clinical governance $150k · GP accreditation and backfill $120k · eConsult platform $110k · independent evaluation $80k.

Months 0–3 · Set up

Build the screen, credential the GPs

  • Complexity Screen calibrated on 12 months of de-identified clinic data
  • GP accreditation module, specialist-led; escalation SLAs signed
  • eConsult platform live; shared-care agreement executed

Months 3–9 · Run

Screen, shift, backstop

  • All referrals + existing book screened; bottom quintile offered the pipeline
  • Weekly MDT; live safety dashboard (escalations, ED representation)
  • Revenue dashboards visible to GPs and specialists alike

Months 9–12 · Prove

Independent evaluation

  • Outcomes vs matched control clinic
  • Economics audited against both ledgers
  • Go / no-go scale decision: next 3 specialties, next 3 networks

07 — Eyes open

The risks, and what holds them

Clinical safety

Risk: a complex patient mis-scored into the pipeline. Safeguard: conservative screen with red-flag auto-exclusions, 24-hour urgent eConsult, and a named specialist of record for every pipeline patient.

Scope drift

Risk: “quintile creep” — the pipeline quietly absorbing more complexity than designed. Safeguard: fixed percentile threshold, quarterly score-vs-outcome audits, annual recalibration.

GP capacity

Risk: enthusiasm outrunning bandwidth. Safeguard: cap of 40 pipeline patients per GP, accreditation before allocation, backfill funding inside the trial budget.

Specialist buy-in

Risk: “you're taking my patients.” Safeguard: the screen removes only bottom-quintile follow-ups — never new or complex work — and the revenue dashboard shows the +$35k, live.

Medico-legal

Risk: ambiguity over who holds responsibility. Safeguard: formal shared-care agreement, documented escalation SLAs, indemnity confirmed for the accredited scope before go-live.

Patient trust

Risk: “I'm being downgraded.” Safeguard: co-signed handover letter — your cardiologist stays in the loop — one-call return path, PREMs tracked from day one.
